Dear Editors,

I submit for consideration in the International Journal of Climatology, as a Research Article, the manuscript "Has the South American low-level jet changed? Radiosonde evidence and the limits set by the observing system, 1980–2025" (single author).

Reanalysis studies report changes in the frequency of the South American low-level jet, but these trends have not been checked against long radiosonde records. Using 46 years of IGRA soundings from 31 stations, together with ERA5, CHIRPS and surface observations, the manuscript shows that the full-profile jet count rises by 2.4 percentage points per decade, that source-archive controls and wind-break adjustments reduce this by 30–40 %, and that the remainder cannot be separated from the 2000–2006 archive transition and a step in the Argentine network. An index of deep jets shows no trend, with a noise sensitivity analysis showing what could be hidden. It also shows that the agreement between ERA5 and the soundings changed abruptly near 1993, so that reanalysis trends over the whole period mix climate and observing-system change. The wider contribution is a methodological one for climatology: how to test a reported circulation change against an observing record whose archives, reporting practice and instruments changed, with explicit limitations (a single reanalysis, a post hoc step date, no reproduction of a published index).

The manuscript is not published and is not under consideration elsewhere. Main text length is about 7,450 words excluding the abstract, declarations, captions and references. Supporting Information (Figures S1–S2, Tables S1–S2 and two sections) and a graphical abstract are provided. The analysis code and intermediate files regenerate all numbers and figures from the downloaded public data. For transparency, I also have a separate manuscript, on a different topic, under review in this journal.
